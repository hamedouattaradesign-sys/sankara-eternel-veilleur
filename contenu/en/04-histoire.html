The story of the project

How the park came to have its work

The Parco Thomas Sankara in Rome is the only public space in Europe given over entirely to the memory of the President of Burkina Faso. It was not born of an institutional decision: it was born of one man's persistence — that of Comrade Madi Sakandé.

A Burkinabè initiative

The monument was carried by Comrade Madi Sakandé, of the Burkinabè diaspora in Italy. He is the donor of the work, to the benefit of the Burkinabè State, for the Parco Thomas Sankara in Rome.

The green space was named by the III Municipio of Rome, in the Montesacro district. The initiative came from Comrade Matteo Pietrosante, the Municipio's councillor for public works and youth policy, and was carried by Paolo Marchionne, president of the III Municipio.

The commemorative plaque was unveiled on Wednesday 17 September 2025, in the presence of Comrade Cyrille Ganou Badolo, Ambassador of Burkina Faso to Italy, and of Massimiliano Smeriglio, culture councillor of Roma Capitale. The plaque recalls that Comrade President Thomas Sankara led Burkina Faso from 1983 to 1987.

In their joint note, the president of the Municipio and the councillor behind the project present Comrade President Sankara as an international reference in the struggle against colonialism, corruption and inequality, and name the values they mean to honour: solidarity, integrity, the emancipation of the popular masses, human dignity and friendship between peoples.

Rome is not a choice of circumstance. Italy is home to the largest Burkinabè community in Europe — more than seventy thousand people, according to the Embassy of Burkina Faso to Italy. It is before them, as much as before Romans, that this monument stands.

Why this is not a statue

I set the figurative statue aside for three reasons.

The place. The Parco Thomas Sankara is an urban park people walk through, not a dedicated memorial site where one comes to stand before an effigy. People pass through it, cut across it, stop in it for a moment. A work legible from every angle belongs there better than a statue with a single face and a single way of being read.

The climate. Corten steel withstands the European climate without maintenance. For a work left in the open air in a public park, out of any close supervision, lasting was a starting condition, not an added quality.

The plastic language. The vocabulary used here — salvaged metal, pressed bosses, volumes drawn from Sudano-Sahelian earthen architecture — is the one I have been developing for more than twenty years in my workshop in Ouagadougou. It makes this monument a Burkinabè work in full, and not a commission carried out in a borrowed style.

From Ouagadougou to Bologna

I designed the work in full, from the sculptural decision through to the vector files for cutting. Those files went to specialist workshops in Italy, which cut the plates by laser and pressed the bosses. The monument was assembled in Rome, at the Parco Thomas Sankara. It carries my signature, cut into the steel at the foot of the portrait face, by the same technique as the face.

A monument complementary to the national Memorial

SANKARA, L'ÉTERNEL VEILLEUR is complementary to, and distinct from, the Thomas Sankara Memorial rising in Burkina Faso. It is neither its replica, nor its extension, nor its foreshadowing, and it replaces it in nothing.

The hearth of that memory is in Ouagadougou: it is there that the Memorial is being built, it is there that the Mausoleum of Comrade President Thomas Sankara and of his twelve companions was unveiled. This monument is a Burkinabè artistic contribution carried abroad, which belongs to the national movement for the restoration of memory led by the authorities of Burkina Faso, under the aegis of the Ministry of Communication, Culture, Arts and Tourism (Ministère de la Communication, de la Culture, des Arts et du Tourisme).
