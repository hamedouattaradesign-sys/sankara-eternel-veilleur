The technique

Two opposite faces carry the portrait of Comrade President Thomas Sankara, obtained by cutting clean through the plate in negative space: it is the voids opened in the steel that make the face, the starred beret, the gaze and the beard. Through those openings the visitor sees the half-darkness inside the monument. The portrait therefore has no colour of its own: it is made of shadow, and it changes with the hour and with where the viewer stands.

The dark areas of the portrait are voids straight through the steel, the light areas are the steel left in place: it is the contrast between metal and daylight that builds the figure, with the sharpness of a great stencil. The side bands framing the face are left smooth.

The two other faces carry no cut at all. They are covered with hemispherical bosses formed in the thickness of the plate itself. The name block ISIDORE NOEL THOMAS SANKARA 1949 - 1987 and the author's signature, 340 by 40 mm at the foot of the face, are cut on the same principle.

Reading the numbers

The geometry of the monument was settled for reasons of proportion and of fabrication. Once the form was fixed, on reading it again, a correspondence appeared: the weave of the work counts those who fell with him, and the day it all began.

  • 12 rows on every face — the twelve companions who fell with him on 15 October 1987.
  • 4 columns of bosses — 4 August 1983, the opening of the Democratic and Popular Revolution. The sculpture itself has four faces.
  • 2 faces carrying the portrait — the two directions, north and south, for the one arriving as much as for the one leaving.

The weave is the same on all four faces: forty-eight places, twelve rows of four. On the two faces that carry the portrait, twenty of those places are given back to the void — sixteen for the face, four for the name block. Where the metal has not been struck, that is him.

Exact count: 48 bosses per textured face — 4 columns × 12 rows —, 28 per portrait face — four in the top row, twenty-four in the lower part —, 152 in all.

“I do not claim to have planned these coincidences. I only observe that when a form is right, the numbers it carries end up saying the same thing it does.” — Hamed Ouattara, statement of intent, Ouagadougou, 17 August 2026.

The story of the project

The Parco Thomas Sankara in Rome was carried by Comrade Madi Sakandé, a Burkinabè settled in Bologna since 1998, of the Burkinabè diaspora in Italy and a former pioneer of the Democratic and Popular Revolution. He committed his own means so that this place should exist and should receive a work, meaning by that gesture to honour the memory of the man he calls his spiritual father. He asked Hamed Ouattara for a work, and not a statue.

The figurative statue was set aside for three reasons. The place is an urban park people walk through, not a dedicated memorial site: a work legible from every angle belongs there better. Corten steel withstands the European climate without maintenance. Finally, the plastic language used — salvaged metal, pressed bosses, volumes drawn from Sudano-Sahelian earthen architecture — is the one developed over more than twenty years in Ouagadougou: it makes this monument a Burkinabè work in full, and not a commission carried out in a borrowed style.

Hamed Ouattara designed the work in full, from the sculptural decision through to the vector files for cutting. The steel plates were worked in Italy by workshops equipped for laser cutting and for pressing the bosses. The assembly, the welding and the finishing were done by the artist in person, in Bologna, before transport and installation in Rome.

A monument complementary to the national Memorial

SANKARA, L'ÉTERNEL VEILLEUR is complementary to, and distinct from, the Thomas Sankara Memorial rising in Burkina Faso. It is neither its replica nor its extension, and it replaces it in nothing. The hearth of that memory remains in Ouagadougou. This monument is a Burkinabè artistic contribution carried abroad, which belongs to the national movement for the restoration of the memory of Comrade President Thomas Sankara, under the aegis of the Ministry of Communication, Culture, Arts and Tourism (Ministère de la Communication, de la Culture, des Arts et du Tourisme).

The author

Hamed Ouattara, whose civil name is OUATTARA Mohamed Sékou, is a Burkinabè multidisciplinary artist designer, a graduate of the École nationale supérieure de création industrielle in Paris. In 2002 he founded the Studio Hamed Ouattara in Ouagadougou, on the Route du SIAO, a workshop organised as a guild with some fifteen craftsmen.

His practice turns salvaged industrial metal into sculptural pieces that carry African memory and identity, in a vocabulary drawn from Sudano-Sahelian earthen architecture.

His works are in the permanent collections of the Victoria and Albert Museum in London, the Centre Pompidou in Paris, the Vitra Design Museum, the Brooklyn Museum and the Denver Art Museum. He is represented exclusively by the Friedman Benda Gallery in New York, has been a Chevalier de l'Ordre du mérite des Arts since 2014, won the Africa Design Award in the same year, and is president of the Association Art Contemporain du Burkina Faso.
